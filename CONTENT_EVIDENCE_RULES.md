# Content Evidence Rules — True Black Café

## Why this exists

The presentation must reflect the team's actual study of True Black Café, Hyderabad. The team visited the café and surveyed/interviewed a server. The available observations have been described as mostly qualitative.

The website must not make qualitative fieldwork look like a quantitative operational audit.

## Evidence categories

Use one of these labels internally for every claim:

| Type | Meaning | How to present |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | Directly witnessed by the team during the visit | “During our visit, we observed…” |
| Interview-reported | A server or staff member stated it | Attribute it: “The server explained…” |
| Report-derived | Explicitly written in the team's report or notes | Preserve the report's wording and meaning |
| External reference | A textbook, official source, or credible external reference | Cite the source and distinguish it from café-specific evidence |
| Illustrative | A hypothetical example or simulation used to explain an OM concept | Label clearly as illustrative; never imply it was measured at the café |

## Never invent

Do not invent:
- Number of customers or orders.
- Peak-hour demand.
- Average waiting time or service time.
- Number of employees, baristas, or workstations.
- Inventory quantities, reorder levels, or stockout frequency.
- Cost, revenue, profit, or wastage.
- Customer satisfaction ratings.
- Productivity, utilization, or efficiency percentages.
- Exact operating procedures not documented by the team.
- Interview quotes or staff statements.
- Recommendations framed as responses to problems that were never observed.

## How to discuss OM concepts responsibly

It is valid to explain concepts such as:
- Process flow.
- Capacity and resource planning.
- Inventory readiness.
- Waiting and bottlenecks.
- Quality consistency.
- Customer experience.

But distinguish the concept from what the team actually verified at the café.

Good:
> “Process flow helps us examine how an order moves from customer request to service. Our field notes document the stages we directly observed.”

Not acceptable without evidence:
> “True Black Café loses 18% productivity because of a 4-minute bottleneck.”

If no measured timings exist, use a process diagram with qualitative stages. If an interactive simulation is included, label all input values and outputs as illustrative assumptions, not café measurements.

## Recommendations

Every recommendation should have:
1. A source-backed observation or a clearly identified general improvement opportunity.
2. The operational principle it relates to.
3. A practical action.
4. A limitation if the team did not measure its expected impact.

Do not claim an improvement will reduce waiting by a percentage unless measured or supported by a transparent, clearly labelled simulation.
